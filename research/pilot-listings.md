## Observations and limits

- Atul Garg: eight of the ten newest entries had the relative date label 'a week ago'; one was three days ago and one two weeks ago. This establishes a concentration within the newest sample, not an abnormal posting rate. The historical baseline and exact date boundaries were not established. One entry was explicitly Google-translated from Hindi.
- Rahul Gupta: the ten newest entries span five days to two months. One reviewer count was absent. Some entries had no visible review text. Do not convert missing count to zero, or absence of text to an authenticity verdict.
- Pradeep Prakash: three newest entries were labelled three days ago; the sample extends to two months. One entry was explicitly marked edited. Owner responses appeared beside customer reviews; their content and dates must not be included in customer-review analysis.
- General praise and similar themes appeared in the displayed material, but no distinctive duplicate passage or coordinated reviewer network was established. Repeated service themes are not the same as evidence of copied wording.
- The user's reported concern is one/two-review profiles, repeated wording, and AI-like language. These remain hypotheses. Review count does not reveal account creation date; polished or translated prose does not establish a fabricated experience.

## Product decision

Recommend a user-activated desktop extension that displays sample observations and traceable pattern evidence. Keep review-count distribution separate from any claim of manipulation. Compare distinctive repeated passages rather than ordinary praise. Only analyze time patterns when coverage and date precision support them. Track original/translated text, truncated/missing text, edited dates, and owner responses explicitly.

Do not output 'fake percentage', corrected star ratings, paid-review allegations, or clinical-quality judgements from this evidence. Lack of detected patterns also does not establish authenticity. These three user-selected cases cannot establish general accuracy.

- Prabal's six low-count reviewers out of eight (75%) exceed the seven out of ten (70%) in the earlier Atul Garg newest sample. Perfect ratings and brief/general praise overlap the proposed weak signals. This does not establish either listing's authenticity; it demonstrates why these signals alone cannot justify separating the user's suspect and credible examples. Business size, specialty, and time coverage also differ.
- Prabal's displayed dates span five months to two years. Two entries share a display name; that does not establish shared identity or coordination. Never merge reviewers by display name.
- Akash Garg's newest sample spans three weeks to three months, including two edited entries. It includes Hinglish, service/pricing complaints, brief praise, and rating-only entries. Its one-star complaint is from a one-review profile: low-count profiles occur in negative feedback too.
- Akash Garg's default relevance-selected material included polished long-form praise from profiles showing 11 and 112 reviews. Writing style and review history do not establish authenticity. Keep polished owner replies separate from customer text.
- Five user-selected Noida-area listings cannot establish how most Indians write reviews or calibrate a fraud classifier. Casual wording, polished prose, grammar, Hinglish, translation, and length must not become authenticity labels.

Refined decision: reviewer-count distribution remains descriptive context with no unvalidated penalty. Prioritize distinctive repeated passages plus corroborating coordination evidence when legitimately available. Preserve mixed patterns within a business. A specific patient story is reading context, not proof of truth. Keep user labels attributed to the user and seek independent evidence before using them as ground truth.

User follow-up: the user identified five Akash Garg reviewers as looking credible, including the one-review critic and reviewers with polished or detailed praise. Four of these examples were visible across the inspected newest/relevance samples; the fifth was not inspected. Their remaining concern is that the volume of positive reviews feels unrealistic. Neither authenticity nor falsity was verified. Review total and positive share cannot establish manipulation without relevant context such as patient volume, listing age, review-request practices, comparable clinics, and longitudinal posting rates. A sentiment distribution is not an independent patient-outcome sample. Do not assign a fraud penalty simply for many positive reviews, or assume that negative reviews are authentic. This follow-up weakens a stylistic classifier as the core product and supports evidence of coordinated behavior as the target instead.

### Implementation candidate

Use the previously recommended WXT + TypeScript + React MV3 stack for the pilot. Keep the core evidence checks local and reproducible. Model-assisted semantic grouping is a candidate only after a representative independently authorized benchmark demonstrates an improvement over simpler comparisons. AI authorship detection is not a substitute for experience authenticity: [2025 evaluation of detector generalization and evasion](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.271/), [2025 study of proficiency and language-environment effects](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1292/).

The data-access restrictions in [feasibility.md](feasibility.md) remain unresolved. Manual inspection proves technical visibility only, not permission for an automated production data pipeline. Resolve that route before implementing the Maps adapter. Evaluation needs comparison cases, independent labels where obtainable, and an unknown category; user suspicion alone must not become the ground truth.
